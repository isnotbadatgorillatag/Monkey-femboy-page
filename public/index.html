Study Finds Minecraft-Playing Monkey Shows Unexpected Preference in Visual Survey

Researchers studying touchscreen choice behavior in a monkey known for playing Minecraft say the animal repeatedly selected one category of images over another.

By F4L News Science Desk • September 2026

A touchscreen-trained monkey known online for playing Minecraft has become the subject of an unusual behavioral experiment examining how primates respond to human faces and visual presentation.

The study focused on Milo, a monkey that researchers say has considerable experience interacting with touchscreen interfaces. During the experiment, Milo was presented with randomized pairs of images depicting girls and femboys. The images were rotated and repositioned between trials to reduce the possibility that the animal was simply responding to a particular location on the display.

Researchers recorded several measures, including the amount of time Milo spent looking toward each image, which image he touched first, and whether he returned to the same category during subsequent trials.

“The consistency of the selections was more interesting than we initially expected,” one researcher said.

Across the fictional trial sessions, Milo selected the femboy images more frequently and spent longer periods interacting with them. The researchers noted that the result should be interpreted cautiously. Visual salience, clothing, colors, facial expressions and other characteristics of individual images can influence an animal's attention without implying any particular social or sexual preference.

A result the researchers did not anticipate

The team said the original purpose of the demonstration was simply to investigate whether a touchscreen-trained primate would make repeatable choices when presented with unfamiliar human faces. The apparent pattern emerged during the later sessions.

After the trials, Milo reportedly returned to his usual touchscreen activities, including Minecraft.

Researchers emphasized that the experiment involved a single animal and cannot be generalized to monkeys as a species. They also cautioned that controlled behavioral experiments require substantially larger samples and carefully standardized stimuli before strong conclusions can be drawn.

For now, Milo's most repeatable behavior remains his apparent ability to navigate a touchscreen well enough to play Minecraft.
